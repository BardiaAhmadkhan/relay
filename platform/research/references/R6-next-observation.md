# Choosing the next useful observation

A new photo has value when it separates explanations that imply different shop actions. A second wide front view cannot tell whether an opening ends in a removable liner. A connected view of both sides and their seam can. A vent calls for an oblique view of the surround, which exposes an edge hidden by a straight-on image.

Golovin, Krause and Ray study noisy tests under a specified prior. Their EC² method values tests by how much probability mass they separate across competing equivalence classes. [Near-Optimal Bayesian Active Learning with Noisy Observations](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2010/hash/1e6e0a04d20f50967c64dac2d639a577-Abstract.html)

Javdani and colleagues extend this idea to overlapping sets of explanations that support acceptable decisions. Their stopping condition and hyperedge-cutting objective address the shop's question more directly than trying to identify every physical detail. [Near Optimal Bayesian Active Learning for Decision Making](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v33/javdani14.html)

Relay enumerates the small documented policy factors and records the smallest sets that can affect the action. For layer, repair and hardware cases the specialist view lists competing explanations, their consequences, one discriminating observation, and an alternative that would add little. It keeps at most one rider request on that screen. A declared exclusion takes precedence, independently of price or stock.

The running templates use known anatomical distinctions. The rate at which a requested view settles a case has not been measured with riders and staff. No statistical prior, calibrated success probability, expected monetary return or approximation guarantee from either paper is assigned to an event garment. A future comparison would randomize the eligible requests within the same unresolved class, count resolved cases per completed request, and track abandoned requests and missed exclusions by physical garment, operator and camera.
